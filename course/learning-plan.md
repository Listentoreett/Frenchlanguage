# One-Month French Learning Plan

## Target

By the end of four weeks, you should be able to:

- introduce yourself clearly.
- handle basic daily life situations in France.
- understand slow, practical French about work, directions, services, and interviews.
- speak with a polite professional tone.
- prepare simple CV, cover-letter, and follow-up language.
- record yourself, compare against IPA and audio, and correct your pronunciation.

This is an A1 to early A2 survival plan with job-search emphasis. The goal is confident useful French, not full fluency.

## Weekly Path

| Week | Focus | Speaking outcome | Listening outcome |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Survival French | greetings, self-introduction, basic needs | recognize slow common phrases |
| 2 | Daily life and workplace | directions, help, availability, team language | follow simple service and workplace exchanges |
| 3 | Job search and interviews | answer recruiter questions | understand job ads and interview prompts |
| 4 | Professional French | follow-up emails, polished answers, formal tone | understand polite business phrases |

## Daily Practice Loop

1. Listen once without reading.
2. Read the phrase, IPA, and sound note.
3. Listen again and shadow immediately.
4. Record yourself.
5. Compare rhythm, vowels, final consonants, and nasal sounds.
6. Repeat from memory.
7. Use the phrase inside a short roleplay.

## Accuracy Rules

- Use `vous` in interviews, offices, shops, services, and with people you do not know.
- Prefer `Je souhaite...` or `Je voudrais...` in professional settings; they sound more polite than `Je veux...`.
- Do not pronounce most final consonants: `petit`, `grand`, `vous`, `parlez`. Some are pronounced: `avec`, `sac`, `hôtel`.
- Watch liaison: `vous avez`, `un emploi`, `c'est à gauche`.
- Do not trust transcript scoring as a true accent score. Speech recognition checks whether the words were understood, not whether the accent is native.

## Weekly Checkpoints

Week 1 checkpoint:

- say a 30-second self-introduction.
- ask for repetition and say you do not understand.
- use `être`, `avoir`, `aller`, and `faire` in the present tense.

Week 2 checkpoint:

- ask for help and directions.
- describe availability and basic work strengths.
- complete a two-person workplace dialogue.

Week 3 checkpoint:

- answer five interview questions.
- explain experience, skills, motivation, and availability.
- write a short application message.

Week 4 checkpoint:

- give a polished one-minute interview answer.
- write a follow-up message after an interview.
- speak formal phrases with calm rhythm and correct liaison.
