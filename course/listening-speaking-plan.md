# Speaking And Listening Plan

## The Daily Cycle

Use this cycle for every phrase in the course.

1. Listen: hear the phrase once without looking at the English.
2. Notice: read the IPA and one pronunciation note.
3. Shadow: play the phrase and speak at the same time.
4. Echo: play, pause, repeat from memory.
5. Record: record yourself once.
6. Compare: check rhythm, vowels, liaison, and final consonants.
7. Use: put the phrase into a realistic answer.

## Listening Drills

Minimal listening:

- Play five phrases from the same week.
- Write what you hear in French.
- Check against the lesson.
- Repeat the two phrases you missed.

Dictation:

- Listen to one sentence three times.
- First pass: write keywords.
- Second pass: complete the sentence.
- Third pass: check accents and apostrophes.

Roleplay listening:

- Ask a tutor or AI assistant to act as a receptionist, recruiter, or colleague.
- Request slow French first.
- Ask for natural speed after two successful rounds.

## Speaking Drills

Pronunciation ladder:

- word -> short phrase -> full sentence -> answer in context.

Example:

- `emploi`
- `un emploi`
- `Je cherche un emploi.`
- `Bonjour, je m'appelle Maya. Je cherche un emploi à Paris.`

Interview answer pattern:

1. Greeting.
2. Identity or background.
3. Experience or skill.
4. Motivation.
5. Availability.

Example:

`Bonjour, je m'appelle Maya. J'ai de l'expérience dans le service client. Je travaille bien en équipe et je suis très motivée par ce poste. Je suis disponible immédiatement.`

## Weekly Speaking Targets

Week 1:

- 30-second self-introduction.
- ask for help, repetition, and clarification.
- speak numbers and time expressions clearly.

Week 2:

- directions dialogue.
- availability paragraph.
- workplace strengths using `je suis` and `je travaille`.

Week 3:

- answer five recruiter questions.
- explain a job ad in simple French.
- record a one-minute mock interview.

Week 4:

- polished interview answer.
- follow-up message read aloud.
- formal phone-style conversation.

## Self-Review Checklist

- Did I keep the French `r` soft and back in the throat?
- Did I distinguish `u` /y/ from `ou` /u/?
- Did I avoid pronouncing silent final consonants?
- Did I make nasal vowels nasal, not followed by `n`?
- Did I use `vous` in professional situations?
- Did I speak in short groups instead of word by word?
