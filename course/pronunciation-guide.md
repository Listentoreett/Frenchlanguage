# French Pronunciation Guide

## Core Sounds

| Sound | IPA | Practice | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| French `r` | /ʁ/ | Paris, travail, merci | make a soft sound at the back of the throat, not an English `r` |
| `u` | /y/ | tu, salut, bureau | say `ee`, then round the lips without moving the tongue |
| `ou` | /u/ | vous, beaucoup, tout | like `oo` in `food` |
| `on` | /ɔ̃/ | bonjour, maison | nasal vowel; air passes partly through the nose |
| `an/en` | /ɑ̃/ | français, restaurant | open nasal vowel, lower than `on` |
| `in/ain/ein` | /ɛ̃/ | demain, plein, intéressant | nasal vowel close to `eh` |
| `eu` | /ø/ or /œ/ | peu, recruteur | rounded lips; keep the sound forward |

## Rhythm

French is syllable-timed. Keep syllables smooth and even:

- `Je suis disponible` -> `je-suis-dis-po-ni-ble`
- `Je vous remercie` -> `je-vous-re-mer-cie`
- `Je peux commencer` -> `je-peux-com-men-cer`

Avoid heavy stress on one word. The final syllable of a phrase is usually a little longer.

## Final Consonants

Often silent:

- `vous` -> /vu/
- `parlez` -> /paʁ.le/
- `motivé` -> /mɔ.ti.ve/
- `français` -> /fʁɑ̃.sɛ/

Often pronounced:

- `avec` -> /a.vɛk/
- `sac` -> /sak/
- `CV` -> /se.ve/
- `poste` -> /pɔst/

## Liaison

Liaison links a normally silent consonant to the next vowel.

| Written | Spoken idea |
| --- | --- |
| vous avez | /vu.za.ve/ |
| un emploi | /œ̃.nɑ̃.plwa/ |
| c'est à gauche | /sɛ.ta ɡoʃ/ |
| dans un bureau | /dɑ̃.zœ̃ by.ʁo/ |

Use liaison especially in common polite and professional phrases. Do not force liaison everywhere.

## Practice Method

1. Say the sound alone.
2. Say it in a short word.
3. Say it in a phrase.
4. Record yourself.
5. Compare only one sound at a time.

Example for `/y/`:

- `u`
- `tu`
- `salut`
- `Je suis ponctuel.`

Example for nasal vowels:

- `/ɔ̃/`: bon, maison, bonjour
- `/ɑ̃/`: dans, restaurant, français
- `/ɛ̃/`: demain, intéressant, entretien
